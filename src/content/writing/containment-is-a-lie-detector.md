A vendor showed me a 74% containment rate last month and I asked what happened to the other 26%. Nobody in the room knew.

That's not a gotcha — it's the normal state of things. Containment is the headline metric for every agentic contact center deployment I've seen, and it's the easiest number in the entire category to flatter without technically lying.

Here's the mechanic. Containment usually means "the conversation ended without reaching a human." A customer whose problem was solved ends without reaching a human. So does a customer who gave up. So does one who hung up and called back forty minutes later from a different number, and one who quietly went to a competitor. All four look identical in the report, and three of them are failures you're counting as wins.

The fix isn't complicated, which is what makes it a little embarrassing that it's rare. Put three numbers next to containment and it stops being able to hide: repeat contact rate within 24 hours, abandonment inside the agent flow, and resolution confirmed after handoff. If containment goes up and repeat contacts go up with it, you didn't deflect anything — you added a step to the customer's day.

What I keep noticing is that the teams who instrument this well aren't the ones with the most sophisticated AI. They're the ones who ran a contact center before they bought one, and who already learned this lesson the expensive way with IVR deflection in 2015. Same trap, better graphics.

The encouraging part: this is a solved problem the moment someone decides to look. Every platform I work in can report those three numbers today. Nobody is blocked on technology. It's a decision about whether you'd rather have a good dashboard or a good contact center, and I think more teams are picking the second one than they were a year ago.

What's the number your contact center reports that you quietly don't trust — and what would it take to replace it?
